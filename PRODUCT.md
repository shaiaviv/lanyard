# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Sales reps at Grain, primarily in two contexts:

**Field (primary surface):** A rep on a busy conference floor with their phone. They've just spoken to someone interesting. They have 30 seconds before the next conversation. They need to log that person without losing the thread.

**Planning (secondary surface):** A rep or sales lead reviewing conference strategy at a desk. They want to prioritize events, check team coverage gaps, and see who they're following up with. Desktop-first, less time pressure.

**Reconcile (in-between):** A rep back at their hotel or desk reviewing the day's captures, deciding whether today's "Sarah at Stripe" is the same person they met at Money20/20 in October.

## Product Purpose

Lanyard is a conference intelligence tool for Grain's sales team. It closes the loop on conferences — from strategy and planning through in-field lead capture to post-event reconciliation and follow-up. The tool covers three experiences: Field (capture), Reconcile (cross-conference contact matching), and Planning (conference strategy hub).

Success looks like: a rep can log a lead in under 10 seconds on a show floor, and later that week know which of their contacts are warm relationships worth closing versus polite tire-kickers they've been chatting with for a year.

## Brand Personality

Sharp · professional · human

Sales-empathy first: warm toward the rep, rigorous toward the data. The tool should feel like a reliable colleague — confident, clear, never fussy. Dark by default, because reps use this at events in ambient light, not in a bright office.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS dashboard** — no light mode, no navy sidebar, no teal-on-white. Not HubSpot. Not Salesforce. This tool earns trust through precision and craft, not institutional familiarity.
- **Dark-hacker aesthetic** — no green-on-black terminal vibes, no cyberpunk decoration, no matrix-rain atmosphere. Professional intelligence, not edgy developer tool.

## Design Principles

1. **Rep-first clarity.** The show floor is loud and fast. Every screen must reduce friction, not add it. If a layout decision could confuse someone at 70% attention, it's wrong.
2. **Data as protagonist.** Names, companies, context, patterns — that's the product. Chrome, decoration, and motion are supporting cast. Remove anything that competes with the information.
3. **Warmth inside the dark.** The amber-gold accent is intentional: professional without cold, precise without clinical. Earned familiarity over sterile minimalism.
4. **State communicates.** Color, motion, and weight carry meaning — not decoration. A button's active state means something. An animation means something changed.
5. **Trust over friction.** Every decision the rep makes on-screen should feel obvious and fast. They should never feel confused about what a screen is asking them to do.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Body text contrast ≥4.5:1 on all backgrounds. The primary use context is a noisy show floor — large touch targets, legible at arm's length, no interaction that requires precision tapping. Reduced motion support is required given animated record button and entrance sequences.
