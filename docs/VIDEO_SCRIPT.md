# 🎥 Lanyard — Submission Video Script (shooting script)

**Format:** AI-avatar narration (HeyGen) over screen-recorded demo, assembled in CapCut.
**Target runtime:** ~8.5–9 minutes (the brief asks for 5–10).
**Voice/tone:** confident, warm, fast-moving founder demoing their own product. First person — the
avatar **is Shai Aviv** (you, the builder), speaking as himself and defending his own decisions. The
video opens by introducing him by name and acknowledging the avatar is AI — a deliberate, on-brand
move for a role about building with AI.

---

## How to use this document

Each scene has three parts:

- **🎬 RECORD** — exactly what to screen-record (the shot list). Record these clips separately; you'll
  lay the narration over them in CapCut.
- **🎙️ NARRATION** — the words the avatar speaks. Paste these blocks straight into HeyGen.
- **⏱️** — running time + notes (where narration plays over live action, where to hold a beat).

**Production notes**
- HeyGen reads ~145 words/minute. Total script ≈ 1,250 words ≈ **~8.5 min** with demo beats.
- Record demos at the seeded **demo login** (`demo@grain.com` / `LanyardDemo!2026`) so every screen
  has real data.
- Record Field clips on a **phone-shaped frame** (or browser mobile viewport, 430px) — it sells the
  "in your hand on the show floor" story. Record Planning/Reconcile on **desktop**.
- Where a clip involves an AI call (voice parse, coverage plan, discovery), **keep the narration
  rolling** over the spinner — the script is timed so you never stare at a loading state in silence.
- Cut to the avatar (talking head) for the *opening*, the *scoring defense*, the *"how I used AI"*
  section, and the *close*. Everything else is screen-share with avatar voiceover (optionally a small
  picture-in-picture avatar in the corner).

# SCENE 1 — Intro + the problem  ·  ⏱️ 0:00–0:45

**🎬 RECORD:** Open on the **avatar talking head** (full frame). Add a **lower-third name graphic** in
CapCut for the first ~4 seconds — *"Shai Aviv · Built Lanyard for Grain"*. Optional: a 2-second B-roll
cutaway of a messy spreadsheet / Slack thread / notebook montage on the word "mess," then back to avatar.

**🎙️ NARRATION:**
> Hi — I'm **Shai Aviv**, and I built everything you're about to see. One quick note up front: the
> version of me talking to you right now is an AI avatar. For a role that's all about building with AI,
> presenting it this way felt like the honest move. Okay — let me show you what I made.
>
> So, the problem. Conferences are one of Grain's biggest sources of new pipeline. But the way they're
> run today is a mess. Which events do we go to? Who covers what? Who did we meet, and did anything ever
> come of it? That lives in spreadsheets, Slack threads, and notebooks — and most of it just evaporates.
>
> So I built **Lanyard**. One app that turns conferences from a gut-feel expense into a measurable
> pipeline engine. It helps the team *decide* which events to attend, *plan* coverage across the year,
> *capture* leads on the floor, *recognize* relationships that build across events, and *sync* the good
> ones into HubSpot. Let me show you — as a salesperson would actually use it.

# SCENE 3 — FIELD: voice capture (the core AI feature)  ·  ⏱️ 0:55–2:05

**🎬 RECORD (phone frame):**
1. Start on the **Capture** screen — the big mic button, conference auto-selected at the top.
2. Tap the mic. Speak a real sentence out loud while recording your screen:
   *"Met Sarah at Stripe, head of payments — really keen on the FX stuff, wants a call next week."*
3. Tap to stop. Let the "Transcribing · Parsing · Matching" state play.
4. Show the **review card** that comes back: name, company, title, topics, temperature, follow-up flag
   all pre-filled. Linger on the confidence-highlighted fields.
5. Tap **commit**. Cut to the **Leads** list showing the new lead landed.

**🎙️ NARRATION** *(this plays over steps 2–5; keep talking through the spinner):*
> Here's the single most important design decision in the whole product. Picture the rep: it's loud,
> they've got thirty seconds before the next conversation, and split attention. If I ask them to tab
> through a form mid-conversation — they just won't. They'll lose the lead.
>
> So the hero action is one button. They talk for ten seconds as they walk away…
> *(beat — let the recording play)*
> …and the AI does the rest. It transcribes the voice note, pulls out name, company, title, topics,
> how warm they were — and scores how well they fit Grain's ICP. A throwaway sentence becomes a fully
> structured, scored lead. A lot of data, captured fast, without stealing a second of conference time.
>
> And notice — this is *why* AI belongs here. It's not bolted on. It's placed at the exact moment a
> human refuses to do the work themselves. Everything downstream depends on this one capture being good.

# SCENE 4 — Cross-conference recognition (the signature feature)  ·  ⏱️ 2:05–3:30

**🎬 RECORD:**
1. **(Phone frame)** Open the **contact arc page** for a recurring contact (one of the seeded recurring
   contacts — pick one with a "Warming" verdict). Show: the verdict chip, the meeting count + time span,
   the **conference trail** (e.g. Money20/20 Europe 2025 → Sibos 2025 → Money20/20 Europe 2026), the
   AI's open threads + suggested next move.
2. **(Desktop)** Cut to **Planning → Relationships** tab. Show the list of cross-conference contacts
   with their verdicts. Apply the **"Warming, no follow-up"** filter so the count drops to the few that
   matter.
3. Find and hover the **"job change" badge** on a contact whose title changed between events (e.g.
   *Director → VP Treasury*).

**🎙️ NARRATION:**
> This is the part the brief weights most heavily, so it's where I put the most thought. When you meet
> the same person across different conferences, Lanyard recognizes it — and it tells you what it *means*.
>
> Because a count is noise. "Met them three times" doesn't tell you anything. The question a rep
> actually has is: *is this going somewhere, or am I being polite to a tire-kicker who's been listening
> for a year and will never buy?* So every relationship gets a verdict based on **movement, not mood** —
> is the engagement *progressing* toward a deal across events? Warming. Nurturing. Cooling. Tire-kicker.
>
> *(over the Relationships tab + filter)*
> And the nudge is calibrated. Too aggressive, it's noise; too subtle, it's invisible. So the headline
> filter is the one that matters most — "warming, but no follow-up scheduled." Rising intent, nothing
> booked. That's a rep's highest-leverage hour, one tap away.
>
> *(over the job-change badge)*
> Two edge cases the brief called out specifically. **Job changes** — when someone's title moves between
> events, Lanyard flags it instead of treating it as a different person. A promotion in your buyer is a
> buying signal, not an error. And **name variations**, which I'll show you next.

# SCENE 5 — RECONCILE: the name-variation edge case  ·  ⏱️ 3:30–4:25

**🎬 RECORD (desktop):**
1. Open **Reconcile** — the inbox of uncertain captures. Point at the count.
2. Open the seeded **"Elena Fisher / Elena Fischer"** resolve card. Show the floor capture on one side,
   the existing contact candidate on the other, the matcher's reasoning, and the low-confidence note
   ("candidates exist but confidence is low").
3. Show the four options: **Match / Create new / Re-analyze (Sonnet) / Skip**. Hover **Re-analyze**.

**🎙️ NARRATION:**
> Out on the floor, I let the matching be fast and forgiving — a wrong hint there is harmless, the rep
> just shrugs it off. But a wrong *committed record* is corrosive. It kills trust, and then the tool
> dies. So capture and commit are deliberately separate surfaces.
>
> Here's a real case I seeded. On the floor someone said *"Elena Fisher, Adyen treasury — think I've met
> her before?"* We already have an *Elena Fisch-er* at Adyen. F-I-S-H-E-R versus F-I-S-C-H-E-R. The
> matcher catches it as a likely match — but it's honest that confidence is low. It will **never
> auto-merge a maybe.** The rep decides: match, create new, or re-analyze with a stronger model — richer
> than the snap judgment the floor could afford. A bad merge corrupts the record forever. A deferred
> decision costs nothing. So I optimize for the cheap mistake.

# SCENE 6 — Scoring methodology, defended  ·  ⏱️ 4:25–5:40

**🎬 RECORD (desktop):**
1. **Planning → Conferences.** Show the ranked list with ICP scores and T1/T2/T3 tiers.
2. Expand one card to reveal the **per-factor breakdown with the AI's one-line rationale**.
3. Open the **weight sliders** panel. **Drag a slider** and show the whole list re-score and re-rank
   instantly. (This is the money shot — do it slowly.)
4. Optionally scroll so the viewer sees treasury/payments events on top, generalist tech at the bottom.

**🎙️ NARRATION** *(consider cutting to avatar talking head for the first two sentences, then back to screen):*
> Now the strategy side. The brief says: rank conferences by ICP fit, pick a methodology, and defend it.
> Here's mine.
>
> Grain's ICP is fintech, payments, treasury, and cross-border FX. The core belief I baked in is
> **quality over headcount.** A six-hundred-person treasury summit full of FX-risk owners is worth more
> to Grain than a seventy-thousand-person generalist tech expo. So every event scores zero to a hundred
> across five factors — and **ICP density is weighted highest, at forty percent.** Raw scale is capped on
> purpose, so headcount can't dominate.
>
> *(over the breakdown + slider drag)*
> And here's what makes it defensible. The AI estimates the fuzzy inputs — "how ICP-dense is this event?"
> — and shows its reasoning on every factor. Nothing's a black box. But a plain, deterministic formula
> turns those inputs into the score. So when I drag a weight slider, the entire list re-ranks instantly,
> with zero AI calls. A team that cares more about geography than topic encodes that in five seconds.
> The AI fills the inputs; the math makes the decision. That same split shows up everywhere in the app.

## Appendix D — Reusable avatar direction (paste into the instruction field for EVERY scene)

HeyGen's prompt-based avatar mode takes a *script* plus an *instruction/direction*. Use the script
from each scene's 🎙️ NARRATION block, and use this **same instruction block, unchanged, for all 12
scenes** — that consistency is what makes the clips read as one continuous person when intercut over
the demo footage.

> **Avatar direction:** A single presenter speaking directly to the camera, framed from the chest up
> and centered, holding steady eye contact with the viewer. This is talking-head narration only — the
> presenter calmly and confidently delivers the script to camera and does nothing else.
>
> Delivery: warm, confident, and natural, like a founder personally walking someone through a product
> they're proud of. Energy slightly up, conversational pacing, clear articulation. Subtle natural facial
> expressions and minimal hand gestures only; relaxed, consistent posture.
>
> Do NOT include any of: scene changes, cuts, camera movement (no zooms, pans, or dolly), background
> motion, transitions, on-screen text or captions, graphics, lower-thirds, props, B-roll, or cutaways.
> No environmental action — just the presenter against a clean, static, uncluttered background with
> consistent lighting.
>
> Keep the avatar, outfit, framing, background, and lighting identical to every other clip. Speak the
> provided script verbatim — do not paraphrase, summarize, add, or omit words. This footage will be
> composited over separate screen-recorded demo content, so it must stay a clean, continuous, single-shot
> talking head.

**Consistency mechanics (these are NOT controlled by the prompt — set them once and reuse):**
- Same **avatar** selection on every scene.
- Same **voice** on every scene (write down its name after picking it on Scene 1).
- Same **background template** — choose a *plain, solid* background (composites cleanly; crops to a PIP
  circle later if you want the avatar in the corner during demos).
- Same **aspect ratio** (16:9 landscape) and framing on every scene.

## Appendix C — Avatar delivery notes for HeyGen

- Paste each scene's NARRATION block as a **separate HeyGen scene/segment** — easier to re-record one
  line without redoing the whole thing.
- Add a short pause (HeyGen supports `[pause]` / punctuation) at every *(beat)* marker so the avatar
  isn't racing the on-screen action.
- Em-dashes are kept light on purpose; if the avatar mis-paces a long sentence, split it at the dash.
- Keep the avatar energy slightly *up* — this is a sales tool; a flat read undersells it.
