The Big Picture
What Lanyard is, what problem it solves, and the organizing idea behind every feature.
One-liner (memorize this)
"Lanyard is a conference intelligence tool with three experiences — Field for show-floor capture, Reconcile for turning uncertain data into confident records, and Planning for company-wide conference strategy. The organizing idea is a relationship lifecycle: Decide → Plan → Capture → Track → Act → Sync."
The Problem (from the brief)

Grain's BD team currently manages conference decisions, coverage, lead capture, and follow-up across spreadsheets, Slack threads, and individual notebooks. Nothing connects the rep on the show floor at Money20/20 to the realization they've now met this same person three times and it's time to close.

Three root problems Lanyard solves:

  • Where to go — no principled scoring of which conferences are worth it
  • Who to meet and remember — no cross-conference identity tracking
  • What to do next — no intelligence turning encounters into action
One App · Three Experiences · One Data Core

Think of it like Gmail: Inbox, Search, Settings are three modes on the same database. Same here.

Defend: "Why one app instead of three separate tools?"
Three separate apps would require sync — a Field capture would need to poll or push before Planning could see it. One shared DB means a capture is instantly visible in Planning with zero sync logic. The rep doesn't manage multiple systems, the ops lead doesn't wait for a sync job, and the relationship arc builds in real time. Coherence is only possible when all three experiences read and write the same tables.
🟦 Field Mobile · Show floor ▾
Rep with a phone, 30s between conversations. Thumb-friendly, voice-first, forgiving of sparse input. Optimized for speed. This is the data producer.
🟣 Reconcile Desktop/tablet · Calm moment ▾
Rep at the hotel or office after the show floor rush. Optimized for correctness. Turns uncertain matches into high-confidence committed records.
🟢 Planning Desktop · Sales lead / ops ▾
Strategic, analytical view. Company-wide coverage, ICP scoring, relationship intelligence, HubSpot sync. Optimized for judgment and overview.
🗄️ Shared Data Core One Supabase DB ▾
Conferences · Contacts · Encounters · Reps · Coverage. All three experiences read and write the same tables. A Field capture is instantly visible in Planning.

Why the Spine matters in the interview
If Ben asks "why did you build X?" — the answer is always "because it's the [stage] step in the relationship lifecycle." Every feature has a reason. Nothing is bolted on. The spine is your defense for the whole structure.
Likely challenge: "Why not just use HubSpot or Notion for this?"
"HubSpot is a system of record. It's built for deals, pipelines, sequences — across every channel. We're a specialized system of engagement for the one context HubSpot does badly: the show floor and cross-conference relationship. We sit upstream, capture and qualify, then push the best leads in. Like Gong or Clay — we feed HubSpot, not replace it."

Core Principles
P1 and P2 are the two principles that settle every design argument. Know them cold — they come up in every feature.
P1 · Be generous with hints, strict with records

The insight: Informing the rep and committing to the system of record are different acts with different stakes. They get different confidence bars.

  • Committing data (confirmed match, encounter history) → requires high confidence. A wrong committed fact is corrosive. Once a rep catches the tool lying, they stop trusting all of it — and an untrusted sales tool is dead.
  • A floor hint → can run on low/medium confidence. The "hint" is the F3 "Met before?" message that appears during capture — e.g. "⚠️ Possibly Sarah Chen, Adyen — met at Money20/20 EU last year." If that's wrong (different Sarah, common name), the rep taps No and moves on. Two seconds lost. The wrong hint costs nothing. A right hint saves the rep from looking lost in front of a returning contact. Asymmetric payoff.
Corollary
Always frame a guess as a guess. "Marco logged a maybe…" not "Marco met this person." Confidence is explicit, not laundered.

P1 across all three experiences:

  • Field — the "Met before?" hint (F3) is low-confidence and clearly framed as such. The committed match in the DB is high-confidence only.
  • Reconcile — the entire experience exists because of P1. Uncertain links from the floor are hints until a human commits them here.
  • Planning — AI Coverage Suggestions is P1 made literal. The suggestion overlays as a visual hint on the timeline and map but never writes a single row to the coverage table. The team's committed travel is the strict record; the AI plan is the generous hint. Same principle, bigger canvas.
P2 · Keep the source, not just the derivation

Store raw inputs — the voice recording, the transcript — not only the AI's parsed interpretation.

  • The source is ground truth: replay it, re-parse with a better model, or audit when a derivation looks wrong
  • You can always re-derive from source; you can never recover source from a lossy derivation
  • First application: retain audioRecording on every Encounter

P2 across all three experiences:

  • Field — raw audio stored in Supabase Storage. If Whisper mis-transcribes, the source is there to replay or re-run.
  • Reconcile — audio playback in the resolve card (the gap we know about — see "What I'd Build Next"). The recording is the rep's own memory of the conversation.
  • Planning — score_breakdown JSONB on every conference stores the AI's raw factor estimates (ICP density: 0.85, topic fit: 0.9…). The 0–100 score is a derivation of those factors. If Grain updates its ICP definition or the weights change, you can re-run computeIcpScore over the stored breakdown instantly — no new AI call needed. The breakdown is the source; the score is the derivation.
P2 in action — the Reconcile use case
In the Reconcile card (R2), the rep can play back the original recording while looking at the match candidates side-by-side. That 15-second audio is often what resolves an uncertain match — "oh right, she said she just moved to Mollie last month" — something the AI parse may have missed or lost. The recording is the rep's own memory of the conversation, surfaced exactly when they need it most: the calm moment after the floor rush, when they're deciding whether two captures are the same person.
How to use this in the interview
P2 isn't just a storage principle — it's a UX decision. The rep captured something in a noisy show floor. Hours later in Reconcile they can't remember if "Sarah Fischer" and "Sarah Chen" are the same person. Playing back their own voice saying "met Sarah from... she said she'd just left Adyen" resolves it instantly. No AI can replace that. The source IS the memory.
Capture vs. Commit — the central architectural decision

Separate capture (fast, in the field) from commit (correct, later).

A captured lead is always saved immediately. Only its link to a known Contact can be provisional (linkState = pending). Uncertainty waits in a holding pen until it can be resolved honestly.

Field = Fast & Forgiving ▾
Capture anything, immediately. AI matches async. Link state = pending if uncertain. Rep moves on. Speed > completeness on the floor.
Reconcile = Slow & Exact ▾
Deliberate, calm moment. High-confidence committed data only. Never corrupt the record with floor guesses. Like a git staging area — draft → confirm.
Defend it: "Why not just commit everything on the floor?"
"Because the floor is the worst place to make identity decisions. You have 30 seconds, noisy environment, the person is right in front of you. If you auto-commit a wrong match, you corrupt the relationship history of a real contact in your system of record. The blast radius of a wrong committed fact is enormous — and it's completely avoidable by queuing uncertain links for a calm moment."

Data Model
Five entities. The Encounter join is the insight that makes everything else possible.
The Core Insight: Contact ≠ Encounter

The most important modeling decision: separate the durable person from each meeting.

  • Contact — the identity we deduplicate. Durable, evolves across encounters. Who they are.
  • Encounter — "Rep X met Contact Y at Conference Z on date D." One row per meeting. Multiple encounters per contact, multiple per conference. What happened.
Why this matters
Without this split, "met them 3 times" is unrepresentable. With it, you can track a relationship across years, conferences, and job changes. This is the foundation for everything: arc summarizer, tire-kicker detection, cross-conference briefings.
Contact — the durable identity
  • linkedinUrl — primary identity anchor by design (verifiable by photo+title at a glance)
  • canonicalName, currentCompany, currentTitle — latest known (NOT per-meeting)
  • email — used for HubSpot dedupe
  • Derived: relationship arc, verdict status

LinkedIn is the anchor because a rep can verify it in 2 seconds (photo + title match the person in front of them). No other identifier is human-verifiable on the show floor.

⚠️ Be honest — this is mocked in the current build
The enrichment provider that surfaces LinkedIn candidates (EnrichmentProvider) is currently a mock — it returns seeded fake profiles unless a real Clay or Apollo API key is configured. The interface, the UI flow (rep taps a profile → fields overwrite ASR), and the architecture are all fully built. The live data isn't wired yet. If Ben asks "can I see LinkedIn profiles in the demo?" — the honest answer is: "The flow is built and the interface is designed for it, but the live enrichment requires a Clay/Apollo key. It's in 'What I'd Build Next' as the first real wiring task."
Encounter — immutable per-meeting record
  • Auto-stamped: conference, rep, timestamp
  • audioRecording — raw voice capture (P2, source of truth)
  • note — transcript / rich context
  • temperature — 5-point warmth ladder (their interest/intent)
  • leadScore — ICP fit (our assessment)
  • topics[] — AI-extracted, e.g. ["FX hedging", "travel merchants"]
  • identity snapshot: name/company/title/email/linkedin as told at this event → a changed title vs. prior = job-change signal, not error
  • linkState — confirmed | pending
The identity snapshot trick
Each Encounter stores who they said they were at that moment. "VP Treasury @Adyen '24 → Head of Payments @Stripe '25" is captured by diffing snapshots — it becomes a job-change signal, surfaced in the relationship briefing.
Defend: "Why not just overwrite with the latest title?"
If we only stored the latest title, a job change would erase the history — you'd never know they'd moved roles. Keeping the snapshot per encounter gives you a time series: diff the snapshots across encounters and a job change surfaces automatically as a relationship signal. "She was VP @Adyen when you first met; now she's at Stripe — she brought the problem with her" is only possible if you kept both snapshots.
Conference, Coverage, Rep
  • Conference — name, dates, location, vertical, audience size, icp_score, tier, score_breakdown JSONB, lat/lng
  • Coverage — "Rep R plans to attend Conference C." Join with status: considering | committed | attended | declined. Distinct from Encounter — a planned attendance is not an actual meeting.
  • Rep — team member. Carries home_city, home_region, capacity for the AI Coverage Suggestions feature. All reps in one shared team pool.
CQRS — reads ≠ writes

Looking someone up (F7 pre-brief) is a read-only operation. It must NOT create an Encounter.

Only an actual capture creates an Encounter. This keeps the encounter history honest — searching for someone doesn't pollute their relationship record with a fake "meeting."

Architecture in 10 Seconds — One App, Three Route Groups

Next.js route groups let you have three distinct experiences — different layouts, different optimizations (mobile vs desktop), different service workers — while sharing one codebase and one database. The parentheses tell Next.js "these are logical groups, not URL segments."

app/ (field)/ ← mobile PWA, offline-first, show-floor capture (reconcile)/ ← desktop, reviewing and confirming pending captures (planning)/ ← desktop, conference strategy and coverage api/ ← AI pipeline routes (server-side only) lib/ ai/ db/ matching/ offline/ enrichment/
Defend: "Why one repo instead of separate apps?"
Separate apps require sync: a Field capture would need to push before Planning could see it. Shared DB means a capture is instantly visible everywhere — zero sync logic, zero drift, no eventual consistency to reason about. The rep on the show floor and the ops lead reviewing the plan read the same Encounter row.
Framework Next.js App Router Full-stack in one repo, no manual endpoints

Next.js is a framework — it's what turns React (just a UI library) into a complete web application with a server, routing, and deployment story. Think of it in layers:

JavaScript ← the language React ← UI library (components, state) Next.js ← framework (routing, server, build) ← this is what Lanyard uses Vercel ← hosting platform

Say "I built it with the Next.js framework" — that single sentence implies React, Node.js, App Router, and Vercel. You don't need to list the layers unless they ask.

App Router is the modern routing system inside Next.js (introduced 2023). Lanyard uses it. The older system was called Pages Router — still works but legacy. When you say "Next.js App Router" you mean: Next.js, using the current routing system.

  • Server Actions — no manual HTTP endpoints: With Vite + Express you'd write every endpoint by hand (app.post('/api/capture', ...)), write a matching fetch() in the frontend, keep the URL strings in sync, and deploy two separate services. With Next.js you write one function marked 'use server' and call it like a normal function from the UI. Next.js generates the endpoint, handles serialization, and wires the connection automatically. Lanyard has ~8 server operations — that's 8 endpoints you never had to write.
  • Route groups — 3 experiences, 1 repo: (field), (reconcile), (planning) are separate layout trees (mobile vs desktop optimized) that share the same DB and lib/ without being separate apps or deployments.
  • Server Components by default: Every component runs on the server unless you add 'use client'. API keys and Claude calls never reach the browser — not by convention, by default.
  • One deployment: git push → Vercel builds and ships both the UI and the server together. No coordinating two separate deploys.
Defend: "Why not Vite + Express?"
Vite is frontend-only. You'd need a full Express server alongside it — separate repo, separate deployment, separate config. Every server operation in Lanyard would require: define the route in Express, write a fetch() in React, match the URL string, handle CORS, keep both in sync. With Next.js Server Actions all of that disappears. For a 4–6 hour project, that boilerplate saving is the difference between shipping and not shipping.
DB / Auth Supabase One service, four requirements
  • Postgres — relational queries for cross-conference matching (fuzzy name search via pg_trgm, complex JOINs). NoSQL alternatives can't express these queries cleanly.
  • Auth — built-in email/OAuth team login. Each user ↔ a reps row. No custom auth to build or maintain.
  • Storage — private recordings bucket for audio files. Audio path stored on the encounter, stream-on-demand from the server. P2 principle: keep the raw capture.
  • Realtime — when a teammate captures a lead, the list updates live without polling. Optional but zero cost to add since Supabase already provides it.
  • RLS (Row-Level Security) — Postgres policies enforce that authenticated users only read/write their team's rows. Security is in the DB, not just application code.
Defend: "Why not Firebase?"
Firebase is document-based (Firestore). Our matching engine needs fuzzy full-text search, trigram similarity scoring, and multi-table JOINs. You can't do that well in a document store. Supabase is real Postgres — same queries, same indexes, same pgvector extension for semantic search when we need it.
Field Delivery PWA — Progressive Web App Offline-first, no app store

Show floors are the worst network environments imaginable — 5,000 people in a hall, all on shared WiFi. Lanyard's core value proposition (capture leads on the floor) must work with zero connection.

  • IndexedDB queue — captures are written locally first, instantly. No network dependency at the moment of capture.
  • Background sync — when connection returns, the queue drains into lib/capture/processCapture automatically. Rep never has to think about it.
  • Service worker — caches app shell so the UI loads even without network.
  • Installable — reps "Add to Home Screen" for full-screen mobile; no App Store / Play Store approval delay.
Defend: "Why not React Native?"
Brief constraint: "a non-developer should be able to host and update this." Native apps require Xcode/Android Studio builds, app store submissions, and review cycles for every update. A PWA is a URL — update by pushing to Vercel. Zero deploy friction for a non-dev.
Why the PWA only installs as the Field app — intentional
The manifest sets "start_url": "/capture" deliberately. No matter what URL a rep installs from, the home screen shortcut always opens Field. Planning and Reconcile are desktop-first experiences — there's no reason to pin them to a phone. One PWA, one job: fast show-floor capture.
AI Layer Claude via Vercel AI SDK + Zod Two-tier model strategy with typed output contracts

All LLM calls go through @ai-sdk/anthropic (the Vercel AI SDK). The SDK normalizes the API surface so you can swap providers without touching call sites.

Haiku — high-volume / cheap
Parse voice captures · Match candidate scoring · Initial ICP fit on every encounter. These run on every save, so cost matters.
Sonnet — lower-volume / rich
Relationship arc briefs · Conference discovery · Follow-up email drafts. Run on demand; quality matters more than cost.

Zod pattern — the critical detail: Every AI call returns structured output validated against a Zod schema. The model is forced to produce typed JSON. If it deviates, the SDK retries automatically.

  • No free-text parsing anywhere in the codebase. AI output is always a typed object.
  • Uses generateText + Output.object() — not generateObject (deprecated in AI SDK v6).
  • EXEMPLAR: lib/ai/parseCapture.ts — every other AI module copies this pattern.
Defend: "Why force Zod schemas on every AI call?"
LLMs hallucinate. Without a schema contract, a "hot" temperature field might come back as "very warm" in one call and "enthusiastic" in the next — uncategorizable downstream. Zod forces the model to retry until it returns exactly "hot" | "warm" | "lukewarm" | "cool" | "cold". Reliability at the type boundary is non-negotiable for a tool that aggregates data over months.
Security Two-Tier Key Architecture Env (infra) vs. in-app Settings (service keys)

API keys are split into two distinct tiers, each stored differently:

  • Tier 1 — env vars (infra): Supabase URL + anon + service-role keys. These bootstrap the database itself — they literally can't be stored in the DB (chicken-and-egg). Set once at deploy by the host. NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL, SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY, etc.
  • Tier 2 — in-app Settings (service keys): Anthropic, OpenAI/Whisper, HubSpot, enrichment provider. Pasted into the UI by an admin, stored encrypted in Supabase, read server-side via getServiceKey(name). Never exposed to the browser. Schema: app_settings(team_id, key_name, key_ciphertext, updated_by).

Why it matters: The brief says "non-developer should be able to host/update this." A non-dev can paste an Anthropic key into the settings UI without touching a .env file or a Vercel dashboard. This is the whole point — operational independence from engineering.

First-run experience
Until service keys are configured, AI features show "Configure API keys in Settings" — the app degrades gracefully to manual mode. No crash, no blank screen, no confusing empty state. The enrichment provider also has a MockEnrichmentProvider fallback so the full flow demos without a live Clay/Apollo key.
AI Speech OpenAI Whisper Speech-to-text for voice capture

The voice capture path (rep hits Record, speaks a quick note about the person they just met) routes audio through Whisper before any Claude processing.

  • Why Whisper: Best-in-class STT quality, especially for proper nouns (company names, titles) which are exactly what a sales rep is saying: "Sarah Chen, VP of Payments, Stripe."
  • Why not Claude: Claude has no audio/speech API. It's text-in, text-out only.
  • Pipeline: audio → Whisper → transcript → Claude Haiku's parseCapture → structured encounter fields.
  • Fallback: The voice path is layered on top of the manual text path. If Whisper fails or the key isn't set, the rep types. The UI degrades gracefully.
What to Remember for the Interview
  • Every tool choice maps to a specific constraint — Vercel = brief requirement, PWA = show-floor wifi, Supabase = 4 requirements in 1 service, two-tier keys = non-dev ops.
  • The route group architecture ((field) / (reconcile) / (planning)) is the answer to "how do you serve mobile and desktop without two apps."
  • Zod is not just validation — it's the AI reliability contract. No Zod = free-text AI output = unmergeable data over time.
  • Service keys in Settings, not env — this is the non-dev hosting story. Say it explicitly if asked about deployment.

TypeScript vs JavaScript

TypeScript wasn't a "should we?" question — it was assumed from day one. Here's why it matters specifically for Lanyard, not just generally:

  • Zod schemas infer TypeScript types: Define a Zod schema once → TypeScript type is auto-generated. No duplicated type definitions.
  • AI SDK output is typed end-to-end: The Zod schema passed to the AI call becomes the TypeScript return type. The compiler knows what shape Claude will return.
  • Type gate on every build: npx tsc --noEmit runs before deployment. Type errors block the build — you can't accidentally ship broken AI output handling.
  • DB types flow through the app: Supabase generates TypeScript types from the schema. A column rename in the DB is a compile error in the app — the type system catches the mismatch before production.
The chain: DB → Zod → TypeScript → AI → React
Supabase schema generates DB types → Zod schemas define AI contracts → TypeScript connects them → React components receive typed props. Break any link in this chain and the compiler tells you immediately. This is what "end-to-end type safety" means in practice.

The Matching Engine
The hardest part — conceptually. Cross-conference identity recognition with graceful handling of name variations, job changes, and cross-rep encounters.
The fundamental tradeoff
False split (miss a repeat — silently fails, worst outcome) vs. false merge (fuse two people — embarrassing wrong nudges). We lean toward recall (catch more repeats) but guard merges with confirmation. Asymmetric blast radius.

Edge Cases — Know All Four
ASR Mangling▾
"Adyen" → "AD in". The matcher is designed to absorb this — it's just another name variation. Fuzzy retrieval catches phonetic similarity. If LinkedIn confirmed: profile fields overwrite garbled ASR output entirely. One feature's weakness is another feature's job.
Job Changes▾
"VP Treasury @Adyen '24 → Head of Payments @Stripe '25." The LLM specifically detects this (jobChange:true in output). Identity snapshot per Encounter captures it as signal. Surfaced in briefing: "Promoted since last meeting."
Cross-Rep Encounters▾
If a colleague (Marco) met this person: system INFORMS you richly ("Marco logged a maybe here") but NEVER auto-merges. You can even ask the prospect to confirm. Linking only happens in Reconcile. P1 in action: "weakest link, don't launder."
Same-Event Duplicates▾
Logging someone already captured today (rep forgot). Detected via My Leads + matcher. Offer "add another encounter" (fully supported — multiple Encounters per Contact per conference) or "update the earlier one." Default: add new Encounter.
The question you'll get: "How do you handle false merges?"
"Two things. First, the confidence threshold for auto-match is high — only your own firsthand repeat at high confidence auto-links. Anything else prompts. Second, linkState = pending means a wrong link hasn't contaminated the system of record yet. It goes to Reconcile, where a human with full context (audio playback, LinkedIn side-by-side, engine reasoning) makes the call. P1: be strict with records."
Signal Reliability Hierarchy

When matching, signal quality matters:

  1. LinkedIn URL — near-deterministic, survives job changes, human-verifiable
  2. Email — strong, but not always available on the floor
  3. Name + company — common, breaks on job change
  4. Name + topics — weaker but useful for niche contexts
  5. Name alone — weakest, many false positives

Reconcile Experience
The correctness layer. Turning uncertain floor matches into confident committed records. "Strict with records" made literal.
The role of Reconcile
Reconcile is mostly assembly — P1 + P2 + the matching engine converge here. It's where "pending" links get resolved into "confirmed." No new machinery, just deliberate humans with full context.
R1 · Pending Inbox
  • List of pending encounters (count, progress, match-confidence badges)
  • End-of-day nudge deep-links here from Field F8 ("N unresolved contacts")
  • Satisfying empty state when all resolved
  • Scope: pending field items only (one rep's own captures, bounded ~10–30 per conference)
Why one-at-a-time inbox (not bulk)
Volume is bounded (one conference, one rep = ~10-30 items). One-at-a-time keeps "strict with records" deliberate — friction is a FEATURE on the surface where being wrong is corrosive. Bulk review invites rubber-stamping.
R2 · Resolve Card

Full context for a confident decision:

  • This capture: transcript, note, temperature, topics
  • Audio playback (P2 — replay the original recording). This is the practical payoff of P2: the rep's own voice, captured on the show floor, becomes the tie-breaker when they're uncertain in Reconcile hours later.
  • Candidate(s): existing contact(s) with their full prior encounter history
  • LinkedIn side-by-side: visual comparison
  • "Re-analyze with Sonnet" — re-runs the matcher at full quality (Sonnet vs. Haiku on the floor). The richer model for a deliberate moment.
Defend: "Why Haiku on the floor and Sonnet in Reconcile?"
Field has two constraints Reconcile doesn't: time pressure (rep is waiting) and volume (20+ captures per conference). Haiku is fast and cheap — the right tradeoff when you need a quick hint, not a definitive answer. Reconcile has zero time pressure and low volume (10–30 pending items). Sonnet costs 5× more but gets the match right more often — and here, getting it right is the whole point. Same matcher function, different model argument.

Four outcomes:

  • Match → linkState flips pending → confirmed (atomic DB transaction — no half-links)
  • New Contact → create fresh Contact row
  • Loop In → flag colleague (cross-rep: link shared Contact + create notification row)
  • Defer → stay pending (rare)
Defend: "Why a separate Reconcile experience at all?"
"Because the show floor and the hotel room require different UX and different confidence bars. On the floor: 30 seconds, noisy, context-limited. In a calm moment: full context, audio playback, Sonnet-quality reasoning. Forcing both into one experience either slows capture or rushes commits. Separation is what makes both great."

ICP Scoring Methodology
The marquee feature. You must defend every number and design choice. Know this cold.
The core design: AI estimates inputs · formula computes score
AI does the fuzzy reasoning (what's the ICP density at this event? how topic-relevant is it?). A transparent, tunable formula turns those estimates into a 0–100 score and tier. Drag a slider → instant re-rank, zero AI calls. The user sees and can modify every input.
The Five Factors (memorize weights and rationale)
FactorDefaultWhy this weight
ICP Density40%How many PSPs/treasury/FX desks are in the room. Quality > headcount. The most important factor — a 600-person treasury summit beats a 40k generic expo.
Topic Fit25%Is FX/cross-border payments the primary agenda, or a side track? A payments-themed conference where FX is a keynote topic beats a "fintech" conference where it's a 20-min slot.
Scale15%Raw audience size, but capped influence. Scale amplifies density; it can't replace it. Highest weight when combined with high density.
Geo Relevance10%EU is Grain's primary market. APAC/MEA weighted lower. Reflects actual travel ROI.
Historical Perf.10%Repeat ROI from previous years. Redistributed to ICP Density when null (new events aren't penalized for having no track record).

Defend: "Why these weights specifically?"
"ICP Density at 40% because quality of audience completely dominates quantity for Grain's sales cycle — a relationship-heavy B2B FX sale needs the RIGHT people in the room, not just many people. Scale at 15% because a 70k generic tech conference at 15% still outsizes a 500-person FX summit at scale — but density at 40% correctly overrides it. These are defensible starting defaults; the whole point of tunable sliders is that a sales leader can adjust them based on what's worked historically."
Where AI runs vs. where the formula runs

AI (Sonnet) runs once per conference — triggered in two places:

  • Conference list (C2) — each card has a "Re-score with AI" button in the expanded view
  • Conference detail (C3) — "Re-score with AI" button next to the score breakdown

Both call the same rescoreConference server action → scoreConference() → updates score_breakdown in the DB. When to use it: you added more detail to a conference, this year's edition changed focus, or you updated the ICP definition and want fresh estimates.

Formula runs everywhere else — slider drag, page load, any weight change. No AI call, pure client-side math over the stored score_breakdown.

The Formula (know this cold)

score = Σ(factor_score × weight) / Σ(active_weights)

Key detail — null redistribution: historicalPerf is null for brand-new events (AI is told not to invent a track record). Its 10% weight is dropped and the remaining factors are normalized against 90 — so a first-time event competes on 4 factors without being penalized for having no history. The formula auto-adjusts; no special case needed in the code.

Two separate functions, deliberately:

  • scoreConference() — server-only, Sonnet, runs ONCE when a conference is added or re-scored. Estimates the fuzzy factor inputs and stores them in score_breakdown JSONB.
  • computeIcpScore(breakdown, weights) — pure, isomorphic JS (no server-only), no AI. Runs on every slider drag, client-side. Takes the stored breakdown + current weights → 0–100 score + tier badge. Instant, free.
Shared ICP Definition — one file, two uses

The ICP criteria live in lib/ai/icp.ts as a single exported string (GRAIN_ICP). It's injected into the system prompt for two completely different AI calls:

  • Conference scoring (C4) — scoreConference uses it to judge: "does this event attract Grain's ICP?"
  • Lead qualification (Field #4) — parseCapture uses it to judge: "is this person Grain's ICP?"

The result: a conference scored T1 and a contact scored 85 are using the same yardstick. "We met this person at a T1 conference" is a coherent signal — both judgments came from the same ICP definition.

Per-factor AI rationale + user overrides

Every AI factor estimate comes with a one-line rationale stored alongside the score in score_breakdown. On the conference detail screen (C3), you see the breakdown panel — not just "ICP Density: 82" but also the AI's reasoning: "Strong PSP/treasury density; Money20/20 is known for decision-maker concentration in payments."

Factor overrides (P1 in action): A rep who attended the event last year can override any factor input — e.g. "AI estimated ICP density at 80 but the floor felt more like fintech generalists, I'd say 55." The override recomputes the score immediately. The AI's estimate is labeled as a guess; the human's override is labeled as firsthand. This is P1 (generous with hints, strict with records) applied to the scoring layer.

Defend: "Why not just use AI end-to-end for scoring?"
"Pure AI is a black box — you can't explain WHY conference X scored higher than Y, you can't tune it, and you can't run it live as a user drags a slider. Pure manual scoring is unscalable and subjective for 190 events. The hybrid is honest about the division of labor: AI handles the inputs it's genuinely better at (fuzzy judgment about event character), a transparent formula handles the aggregation. The breakdown is fully auditable — every number has a rationale."
Defend: "Why is cost not in the score?"
"Fit and cost are orthogonal axes — a high-fit expensive conference and a high-fit cheap one both deserve T1 status. If you mix cost into the fit score, you corrupt the signal. The right model is a 2×2: ICP fit on one axis, cost/effort on the other. High-fit + expensive = invest selectively. High-fit + cheap = no-brainer. Low-fit + cheap = still a waste of time. Keeping them separate means the prioritization logic stays clean."

AI
All AI Features — Why Each One Is Justified
The rubric is "AI judgment, not bolted-on." For each feature: remove it and the core value prop breaks, or the job requires world knowledge / synthesis that rules can't do.
1 · Voice Capture → Parse + Fit Scoring

Why AI is right: The rep speaks 15 seconds of unstructured speech. You can't extract name/company/title/topics/temperature/fit/follow-up from that with rules. The LLM is the parsing layer that turns speech into structure — and it flags its own uncertainty via confidencePerField.

Why not bolted on: Remove it and the core Field value prop (voice-first capture) doesn't work. AI is load-bearing.

Cost control: Haiku (cheap model). Parse + fit in ONE LLM call (zero incremental cost for fit).

2 · Relationship Arc Summarizer

Why AI is right: Synthesizing 5 encounters across 3 conferences over 18 months into "Last time at Sibos she asked for a pricing example, never got it. She's now VP. Lead with that." requires reasoning over messy, variable-length text. Rules can't do this.

Why not bolted on: Knowing "3 encounters" is a number. Knowing "warming relationship with an unmet ask — close now" is the insight. The value of cross-conference tracking is useless without interpretation.

Model: Sonnet (deliberate and high-quality; this is the briefing a rep stakes a conversation on).

3 · ICP Scoring Engine (AI Estimates Factors)

Why AI is right: "What % of Money20/20 Europe attendees are PSPs or platforms with FX exposure?" isn't in a spreadsheet. The LLM knows the conference world and can estimate ICP density, decision-maker presence, and topic alignment from public knowledge.

Why not bolted on: The alternative is a human manually researching 190 conferences. AI handles the fuzzy estimation; the transparent formula handles the scoring. Division of labor is honest and auditable.

Important design: AI estimates factors ONCE (stored), formula recomputes LIVE (no AI call on slider drag). Respects both the UX and the credit budget.

4 · Conference Discovery

Why AI is right: Finding conferences you don't already know about — the brief's named example feature. "What payments/fintech/FX events should Grain's BD team know about in APAC in H2 2026?" requires world knowledge + synthesis. A search engine gives you links; the LLM gives you structured, pre-scored candidates.

Why not a hand-curated list: A manual list is subjective, stale after one quarter, and can't find events you didn't think to search for. The LLM surfaces unknowns based on your ICP definition — events that match Grain's criteria but aren't on anyone's radar yet. Then the C4 formula auto-scores them, so discovered events arrive already tiered. The counterfactual (a person spending a day researching APAC treasury conferences) doesn't scale to 190 events across 12 regions.

Workflow: AI generates candidates → each is auto-scored via the C4 formula → user reviews and adds. Same hybrid pipeline, consistent methodology.

Mock fallback: 4 real pre-baked candidates when no API key. Feature demos offline.

5 · AI Coverage Suggestions

Why AI is right: "Given 6 reps in different home cities, 190 conferences, committed travel, and the instruction 'prioritize EU treasury' — allocate who goes where." This is a global soft-optimization over natural-language goals. Deterministic code can enforce hard constraints; only AI can interpret the instruction and balance soft objectives (clustering, fairness, ICP coverage).

Why not bolted on: This replaces a multi-hour planning exercise. The counterfactual (manual spreadsheet) is obvious and painful.

The "AI judgment" test — apply to any feature
Ask: does this job require (a) world knowledge, (b) synthesis over messy unstructured inputs, or (c) soft judgment that rules can't encode? If yes to any → AI is right. If it's deterministic, computable, or rules-based → DON'T use AI (see: the formula recomputing on slider drag).

The Three Hardest Questions + Short Answers
"How do you prevent false merges?"
Auto-match only at high confidence + your own firsthand repeat. Everything else prompts or queues to Reconcile as pending. Pending = not yet in the system of record. Human with full context (audio playback, LinkedIn, Sonnet re-run) makes the call.
"Why is Web Summit a T3?"
70k attendees but ~95% are non-ICP (general tech/startup). ICP Density at 40% of the score correctly overrides scale. A 600-person treasury summit beats it. Scale without fit is noise, not signal.
"Why separate Reconcile from Field?"
Different contexts demand different UX and different confidence bars. On the floor: 30 seconds, noisy, context-limited. At the hotel: full context, audio playback, Sonnet quality. Forcing both into one experience either slows capture or rushes commits.

⚡ Page Caching — Quick Win, Big Perceived Speed Gain

What's slow: Every page navigation hits Supabase fresh — no caching. The planning page fires 8 parallel DB queries on every render (conferences, coverage, follow-ups, relationships, gap analysis, reps, weights, suggestions). Combined with Vercel cold starts on the free tier, simple page transitions feel slow.

The fix: Add 'use cache' (Next.js 15 directive) to the heavy read queries in lib/db/queries.ts. Wrap getConferences(), getReps(), getScoringWeights() — data that changes rarely — with a 60-second cache. Subsequent navigations return instantly from memory; no DB round-trip.

Why it wasn't done from the start: Deliberate tradeoff. Caching adds complexity around invalidation — if you cache the conference list but someone adds a conference, the cache must bust correctly or you show stale data. Getting that wrong silently is worse than slow. During a time-constrained build, correctness first was the right call. This is a 30-minute task now that the data model is stable.

On paid Vercel: Warmed function instances eliminate cold starts entirely — that alone would fix most of the perceived slowness without any code change.

🎙️ Audio Playback in Reconcile — Known Gap, High Value

What's missing: P2 (keep the source) says we store every voice recording in Supabase Storage. The Reconcile card shows the transcript and match candidates — but there's no way to actually play back the recording. The UI was never built.

Why it matters: The recording is the rep's own memory of the conversation. In Reconcile, hours after the show floor, a rep staring at two possible "Sarah" matches can hit play and hear their own voice say "she mentioned she just moved to Mollie last month" — that 15 seconds resolves the uncertainty instantly. No AI prompt can substitute for that.

How to build it: Fetch a signed URL for encounter.audio_path from Supabase Storage, render a native <audio> element in the Reconcile card. Small addition, high payoff.

Why this is the first thing I'd ship
It's the gap between P2 as a principle and P2 as a product feature. The data is already there — the storage, the path, the principle. The UI is one component away. High leverage, low effort.
↩️ HubSpot Inbound Pull — "Is This Already a Customer?"

What's missing: The integration is one-way (us → HubSpot). There's no check on the way IN: when a rep captures someone in the field, we don't know if that person is already a Grain customer, an open deal, or a churned account.

Why it matters: A rep pitching the Grain product to someone who's already a customer is an embarrassing failure mode. A rep who gets a briefing saying "⚠️ Open deal — Sarah is already in a trial with the payments team" walks into that conversation completely differently.

What it would look like: On capture (or pre-brief in F7), query HubSpot by email → surface a banner if the contact already exists as a customer, open deal, or churned account. Read-only lookup, no sync complexity.

📊 Outcome Tracking — Close the Measurement Loop

What's missing: Right now historicalPerf is almost always null — the AI is told not to invent a track record, so it gets dropped and the other 4 factors absorb its weight. There's no way to feed real results back into the scoring model.

What it would look like: After a conference ends, the rep (or a HubSpot sync) logs meetings booked and pipeline generated. That data writes back to historicalPerf for that conference. Next time it's scored, the factor is real — based on actual Grain results, not the AI's world knowledge guess.

Why it matters for this role: The job description measures impact by "meetings booked, pipeline generated, and time saved." This feature closes that loop — you can prove EuroFinance generated 3 pipeline opportunities last year, so it scores higher next year. The tool learns from real results instead of estimating indefinitely.

Why it's deferred: You need at least one full conference cycle of data before it's meaningful. The infrastructure is already designed for it — historicalPerf is a first-class factor in the schema, just waiting for real inputs. The input mechanism is the missing piece.

🔄 Live Conference Enrichment — Continuous, Not Point-in-Time

What's missing: The 190-conference database was built with a one-time research pass. Dates, venues, and speaker lineups change. New events are announced constantly. Right now it's a static seed.

What's next: A background agent (cron job) that periodically re-runs the discovery flow, flags new ICP-relevant events, and surfaces estimated-date events for confirmation when organizers publish real dates. Keep the database fresh automatically.

Why it matters: A sales intelligence tool that goes stale after deployment isn't a tool — it's a spreadsheet. The brief asks for something the team actually uses daily. Continuous intelligence is what makes that possible.

👥 Multi-Person Capture

What's missing: Currently one recording = one person. In reality, reps often walk out of a group conversation having met 2–3 people at once.

What's next: When the parse step detects multiple names in the transcript, offer to split into separate Encounters automatically, or flag for the rep to log a second person. The AI already extracts names — routing them to separate captures is the addition.

Why it's deferred: The MVP assumption (1 recording = 1 person) was deliberate — don't half-build a complex flow. Get the single-person path perfect first. This is the natural next step once that's stable.

📝 Coverage Suggestion Rationale — Zod Validation Gap

What happens: Occasionally Sonnet returns an empty string for the rationale and an empty array for perRepNotes — the assignments come back fine but the narrative is blank. The suggestion gets saved and displayed with no explanation.

Why it slips through: Zod's z.string() only checks that the value is a string — it doesn't care if it's empty. "" passes validation silently. The fix is z.string().min(1), which would force a retry instead of saving a blank rationale.

How common: 2 out of 5 suggestions in the DB had this — both from early test runs with very few assignments. The full-scale suggestions all produced good narratives. Likely happens when the input is minimal and Sonnet doesn't bother filling in the narrative fields.

The fix: Tighten the Zod schema to z.string().min(50) for rationale and z.array(...).min(1) for perRepNotes. That forces a retry on any run that returns empty narrative fields.

🔁 HubSpot Push Doesn't Clear the Follow-Up Queue

What happens: Pushing a contact to HubSpot doesn't remove them from the follow-up list. The follow_up flag stays true on the encounter, so the contact keeps sitting in the queue after being pushed. The "In HubSpot" button state is local React state only — on refresh it resets to "Push to HubSpot."

With a real API key: The push does stamp provenance.hubspot.pushed_at in the DB — so the button could read that on mount and show "In HubSpot" persistently. That logic isn't built yet.

The mock: No API key → mock fallback simulates a push, shows "Pushed (demo)" — intentionally changes nothing in the DB. That's correct behavior for a demo.

What it should do: After a successful real push, either auto-unflag the follow-up (follow_up = false) or show a persistent "Pushed ✓" state by reading provenance.hubspot on load. Both are small additions.

❌ Dismiss from Follow-Up Queue — Missing Inline Action

What's missing: The follow-up queue only has one action: push to HubSpot. There's no way to say "not worth chasing" and clear a contact from the queue inline. To remove someone a rep has to navigate to the encounter record and manually uncheck "Flag for follow-up" — which nobody will do mid-queue review.

Why it matters: A queue you can't clear is a queue reps stop trusting. If dismissing a low-quality lead takes 4 clicks and a page navigation, reps will just leave them there — and the queue becomes noise, not signal.

What it would look like: A "Not worth it" or "Dismiss" button next to each follow-up card — one click, sets follow_up = false on the encounter, removes it from the list instantly. Optionally a quick "why" tag (wrong person, already a customer, no budget) for later analysis.

🗑️ Delete / Undo a Lead

What's missing: There is currently no way to delete or undo a capture. If a rep accidentally logs the wrong person, or taps record twice, the encounter stays in the system with no removal path.

What the plan says: The field edge cases spec covers this — soft-delete the Encounter, and if it created an orphan Contact (no other encounters) clean that up too. The design is clear; it just wasn't built.

What it would look like: A swipe-to-delete or trash icon on the My Leads list (F4) and the contact card (F5), available for a short window after capture ("undo" within the same session). After that, a deliberate delete action in the contact card requiring confirmation.

🔀 Manual Contact Merge — "I Know These Are the Same Person"

What's missing: If the system created two separate Contact records for the same person — and didn't offer to connect them — there is currently no way for the rep to manually merge them. This is explicitly in the foundation's parking lot: "merging existing contacts" was deferred from v1.

Why it happens: The matcher runs at capture time. If the rep captured someone twice at different conferences with slightly different spellings, and both times the match confidence was low (so both went to New Contact), you now have a split identity. The rep knows they're the same person. The system doesn't.

What it would look like: A "Merge with…" action in the contact card — search for the duplicate, preview the combined encounter history, confirm. All encounters from both records get reattached to a single Contact. Lives naturally in the Reconcile experience as a proactive integrity tool, not just a pending-captures inbox.

Why it's in the parking lot and not a gap
It was a deliberate scope cut, not an oversight. The Reconcile experience handles uncertain captures before they're committed. Merging already-confirmed records is a different, heavier operation — it touches committed data, which is P1's strict side. Getting the pending→confirmed flow right first was the right call. This is the natural v2 extension of Reconcile.
🔗 Live LinkedIn Enrichment — The Identity Anchor Gap

What's missing: LinkedIn is described as the primary identity anchor — the one thing that makes cross-conference matching near-certain. But the enrichment provider that surfaces LinkedIn candidate profiles during capture is currently a mock. The flow (rep taps a LinkedIn card → photo+title confirm identity → fields overwrite ASR mangling) is fully built in the UI. The live data feed isn't wired.

What's next: Configure a real Clay or Apollo key in Settings. The EnrichmentProvider interface is already the abstraction layer — switching from mock to real is a settings change, not a code change. Why it's deferred: LinkedIn ToS complexity, Clay/Apollo API cost, and auth were flagged as the T1 highest shipping risk before a line of code was written. The deliberate choice was: build the interface, ship the mock, unblock on a key. It's the right sequencing call — not a shortcut.
